# Quantum Machine Learning in Oncology: A Comparative Evaluation Framework ## 1\. Healthcare Problem Definition ### Clinical Context and High-Impact Relevance Breast cancer represents one of the most pressing challenges in global oncology, accounting for approximately 2.3 million new diagnoses and nearly 1 million deaths annually. In modern clinical practice, therapeutic decisions, risk stratification, and patient outcome forecasting rely heavily on multi-modal clinical, genomic, and molecular profiling. The primary clinical objective established in this investigation is the prediction of overall five-year survival status—a binary classification task differentiating living patients from deceased patients—using high-dimensional multi-omic patient profiles. Accurate prognostic modeling allows oncologists to deliver personalized interventions, adjust chemotherapy regimens, and optimize post-surgical monitoring. ### Data Description and Preprocessing Pipeline To establish a standardized and reproducible computational benchmark, this evaluation utilizes the Molecular Taxonomy of Breast Cancer International Consortium (METABRIC) cohort, comprising detailed clinical and genomic records from 2,509 breast cancer patients. The raw dataset includes 39 clinical attributes alongside high-throughput gene expression measurements. Furthermore, standardized medical benchmarks documented across current literature—such as the Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer (WDBC) dataset for morphological tumor classification, the MedMNIST 2D/3D biomedical image benchmark, and the PTB-XL ECG dataset containing 21,799 clinical records—demonstrate the broad applicability of machine learning across oncological and cardiovascular domains. To eliminate data leakage and ensure methodological rigor, all data transformations are strictly executed within fold-isolated cross-validation partitions: \* \*\*Quality Filtering\*\*: Clinical variables containing over 10% missing values (such as Tumor Stage) are removed. \* \*\*Imputation Protocols\*\*: Continuous numerical features undergo median imputation, while categorical variables are filled using mode imputation. \* \*\*Encoding and Normalization\*\*: Categorical attributes (including PAM50 intrinsic subtypes, hormone receptor status, and treatment categories) undergo one-hot encoding dropping the primary category to prevent multicollinearity. This yields a complete 63-dimensional normalized feature space after applying standard score scaling. ### The Dimensionality Bottleneck and Quantum Qubit Mapping Processing high-dimensional multi-omic features introduces the "curse of dimensionality," where traditional classifiers face exponential memory requirements and risk overfitting. On Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) devices and simulators, memory complexity scales exponentially as $2^n$ with qubit count $n$. Directly simulating a 63-qubit system requires processing over 9 quintillion complex amplitudes, making uncompressed quantum simulation computationally intractable. To bridge this physical hardware constraint, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is fitted on the training split of each fold to compress the feature space into reduced dimensional representations (12 components retaining 56.10% of variance, 4 components retaining 26.78% of variance, and 2 components retaining 16.23% of variance). Each retained principal component is mapped one-to-one onto an individual qubit via angle encoding, establishing a controlled framework for comparative quantum-classical evaluation. --- ## 2\. Quantum Machine Learning Contribution ### Quantum State Preparation and Feature Encoding Quantum machine learning approaches leverage high-dimensional Hilbert spaces to evaluate complex feature relationships, as explored in research from York St John University and Frontiers in AI. To process continuous clinical features on quantum circuits, continuous PCA values are mapped into quantum state vectors using \*\*Angle Encoding\*\*. Each principal component feature $x\_i$ is mapped to a single qubit using parameterized single-qubit Y-axis rotation gates: $$|\\psi(x)\\rangle = \\bigotimes\_{i=1}^{N} RY(x\_i) |0\\rangle^{\\otimes N}$$ Angle encoding maintains a linear circuit depth $O(N)$ and requires no auxiliary ancilla qubits, making it highly suitable for current NISQ execution. ### Quantum Classifier Architectures #### 1\. Variational Quantum Classifier (VQC / QNN) The Variational Quantum Classifier employs a hybrid quantum-classical optimization loop. The architecture consists of three core components: 1\. \*\*State Preparation\*\*: Angle encoding of input components using $RY(x\_i)$ gates. 2\. \*\*Parameterized Variational Circuit\*\*: Entangling layers consisting of single-qubit rotations $RY(\\theta\_{l,q,0})$ and $RZ(\\theta\_{l,q,1})$ interleaved with a circular ring of CNOT gates ($q\_0 \\rightarrow q\_1 \\rightarrow q\_2 \\rightarrow q\_3 \\rightarrow q\_0$) to generate inter-qubit entanglement. 3\. \*\*Measurement and Optimization\*\*: Measuring the Pauli-Z expectation value $\\langle Z\_0 \\rangle$ on the primary qubit to obtain raw logits, converted into class probabilities via a sigmoid activation function. Variational parameters $\\theta$ are updated using finite-difference gradient estimation to minimize binary cross-entropy loss. #### 2\. Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM Kernel) The Quantum Support Vector Machine relies on quantum feature maps to compute non-linear sample inner products in a high-dimensional quantum state space. For input vectors $x\_i$ and $x\_j$, state preparation circuits $\\phi(x\_i)$ and $\\phi(x\_j)$ construct quantum state vectors $|\\phi(x\_i)\\rangle$ and $|\\phi(x\_j)\\rangle$. The quantum fidelity kernel $K(x\_i, x\_j)$ is computed as the transition probability: $$K(x\_i, x\_j) = |\\langle \\phi(x\_i) | \\phi(x\_j) \\rangle|^2$$ The resulting $N \\times N$ Gram matrix is passed into a classical dual C-SVM solver to determine optimal hyperplanes. ### Theoretical Justification and NISQ Relevance QML architectures offer a unique approach to high-dimensional biomedical data by expressing non-linear feature interactions directly through quantum superposition and entanglement without computing explicit higher-order polynomial combinations. Furthermore, parameterized quantum kernels can access feature spaces that are computationally hard for classical kernels to reproduce, addressing the representational limits of classical linear boundaries. --- ## 4\. The Dimensionality vs. Expressivity Trade-Off Comparing quantum classifiers directly against full-feature classical models (96.60% vs. 48.20%) highlights a substantial performance gap. However, as noted in recent literature benchmarking oncological data, this discrepancy is primarily driven by the severe information bottleneck enforced by Principal Component Analysis during downscaling rather than an inherent failure of quantum circuits. When classical models are similarly restricted to reduced principal components (PCA-12), classical performance drops to 65.20%, demonstrating that aggressive feature compression destroys critical predictive variance in tabular multi-omic data. #### 2\. Computational Overhead Execution latency analysis reveals a stark contrast between classical optimization and quantum simulation. While classical models require milliseconds per fold (0.008s for Logistic Regression), state-vector simulation of the 4-qubit Variational Quantum Classifier requires 10.367 seconds per fold due to iterative gradient evaluations across parameterized quantum gates. The Quantum SVM Kernel offers a significantly more efficient quantum approach, completing evaluation in 0.131 seconds per fold while achieving competitive ROC-AUC performance (0.5264). #### 3\. Current Limitations and Methodological Roadmap \* \*\*Information Bottleneck\*\*: Linear feature compression via PCA discards non-linear genomic interactions prior to quantum state encoding. Future work should explore quantum autoencoders or amplitude encoding to preserve higher-dimensional input features without increasing qubit count requirements. \* \*\*Barren Plateaus and Optimization Noise\*\*: Variational circuits face vanishing gradients in parameterized parameter spaces. Incorporating layer-wise pre-training or quantum natural gradient optimizers can improve convergence stability. \* \*\*Real Hardware Deployment\*\*: Executing circuits on physical NISQ hardware introduces environmental decoherence and gate noise, necessitating matrix-free measurement mitigation and dynamical decoupling error suppression techniques, as demonstrated in recent 127-qubit quantum processor studies on MedMNIST. --- ## 5\. Conclusion and Strategic Recommendations This benchmark establishes a transparent framework for evaluating quantum machine learning against established classical baselines in clinical oncology. While classical tree ensembles and linear classifiers remain superior for uncompressed tabular clinical data, quantum kernel approaches and variational circuits demonstrate technical feasibility for low-dimensional representation spaces. To realize a genuine quantum advantage in healthcare, research must transition toward multi-omic datasets that exhibit naturally high quantum entanglement and develop native non-linear feature embedding techniques that bypass classical PCA pre-processing.
